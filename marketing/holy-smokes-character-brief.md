# Design brief: a character for Holy Smokes BBQ Co

For Claude Design. Written 2026-10-01 by the Website session. Copy everything
below the line into Claude Design as the prompt. Joel names the character; the
brief leaves the name blank on purpose.

The light show's elf (Breaker) worked because he came from a real, repeated
problem (the breaker tripping in the snow) and was drawn as a real object
(a blow-mold yard figure). This brief follows the same recipe: a real thing
from the pit, drawn as a real object, with one dry running joke.

---

I need a small recurring character for Holy Smokes BBQ Co, a one-man backyard
barbecue outfit run by a United Methodist pastor in Wisconsin who was born and
raised in the Kansas City area. He makes three free BBQ apps (BBQ Scorecard,
BBQ Notebook, BBQ Board), a website calculator, and a YouTube channel where he
smokes meat without saying a word. The character will sign off website
sections, sit beside a photo now and then, and appear in social posts. It
will never be a big mascot banner.

## Who the character is

A **smoke puff**: a small, rounded cloud of wood smoke that has drifted off
the pit and hangs around because it likes the company. The name of the
company is "Holy Smokes," so the character is literally one of the smokes.
It is patient and a little dry, the kind of presence that has watched a lot
of briskets rest and will tell you to wait. It is never hyper, never a
salesman, never a chef.

The one running joke: it always wants to know what the pit temperature is.
"Still 225?" It carries, or hovers next to, an old analog dial thermometer.

## The big idea: drawn like a hand-painted smokehouse sign

The brand's logo is a round badge: an orange disc with a cream cross, the
words HOLY SMOKES in a tall condensed lettering, and two flares of flame at
the sides, on a charcoal ring. The brand typeface is Thornwood, a heavy
condensed sign-painter's face. The look to match is **painted signage**:
flat color, a single confident outline, simple shapes, a little wear. Think
of a figure painted on the side of a meat market or a smoker trailer, not a
cartoon from a streaming show.

The puff should read as smoke, not as a cloud from a weather app: soft
irregular edges on top, a thinner wisp trailing off the bottom toward where
the fire would be, and a faint warm glow underneath as if lit by coals.

## Face and expression

Two small eyes and a mouth, painted on, nothing more. Default expression:
calm and dry, one eyebrow up a touch. Pleased when a slice is cut. Mildly
alarmed only when the thermometer reads wrong. Avoid big glossy eyes, a wide
open grin, a tongue, or a wink.

## Props (two, no more)

- An **analog dial thermometer** on a short probe, either held in a wisp or
  stuck in the ground beside it.
- A **square of pink butcher paper** it sometimes rests on or holds, for the
  "resting" pose.

No chef hat, no apron, no tongs, no sunglasses, no fire coming out of it, no
cross (the cross is the company's, not the character's).

## Must match the existing brand

- Colors: near-black `#1b1714`, cream `#E6D8B8`, logo orange `#D06129`, gold
  `#F3CF6D`, charcoal `#444947`, ink brown `#33211A`. The smoke itself is
  cream and warm gray; the glow underneath is orange to gold; the outline is
  ink brown or charcoal. The thermometer face is cream with an ink needle.
- Lettering, if any sample text is needed in a mock, is Thornwood. Never
  bake text into the final art.
- Line weight and corner feel should sit comfortably next to the round badge
  logo.

## Deliverables

1. **Line-art version**, full figure, one color (ink brown on transparent,
   plus a cream version for dark backgrounds). This is the main version; it
   must read at 48px tall.
2. **Full-color "lit" version** with the coal glow underneath, for the
   homepage and social posts.
3. **Head-only avatar** in a circle for comment replies and sign-offs,
   readable at 32px.
4. **Signature pose, "Still 225?"**: checking the thermometer, eyebrow up.
5. **Resting pose**: sitting on the butcher paper, eyes half closed,
   for "let it rest" posts.
6. **Expression sheet**: dry, pleased, surprised, patient, skeptical,
   sleepy. Six faces on transparent backgrounds, same size, so they can be
   swapped on the site.

Formats: SVG where possible, plus transparent PNG at 1x and 2x.

## Please avoid

- Anything resembling Smokey Bear, the Kool-Aid Man, Casper, a Pixar cloud,
  or any existing mascot.
- Glossy 3D rendering, sparkle, lens flares, "AI mascot" shine, drop
  shadows on everything.
- Baby proportions, sticker-pack cuteness, or a cowboy hat.
- A flame character. The brand already has flames in the logo; the
  character is the smoke, which is the calmer half of the fire.
- Any text, prices, or app screenshots in the art.

## How it will be used

One sign-off at the bottom of the homepage and one aside beside a photo, at
most, per page. It never sits on top of prices, store buttons, or privacy
text. In social posts it appears small in a corner or at the end of a
caption. The website sets its lines in Joel's voice; the character does not
narrate the site.
